🍵 Bone broth daily

One warm cup at 4 PM every day with a small snack. Want it fully Kerala? Swap in rasam (peppery tamarind broth) on any day.

🌾 Millet bases

Every base is a millet (foxtail, little, ragi), a moong/lentil dosa & idli, or cauliflower rice. Millets digest slowly and keep blood sugar steady.

🥥 Kerala on the plate

Coconut, coconut oil, curry leaves and shallots run through the menu — avial, thoran, olan, kadala curry, and Kerala-style fish & chicken curry.

🫘 Beans, lentils & dals

Lentils and dals just need rinsing (soak 1–2 hrs only for grinding dosa). Dried kadala (black chickpeas), cowpeas or other beans: soak overnight the day before, then drain and throw away the soaking water. Canned: just rinse.

🥤 Drinks & snacks between meals

Water all day. Plain coffee/tea or Kerala sambaram (spiced buttermilk, no sugar). If hungry between meals: cucumber or roasted chana.

Make it stick

Tips for reversing insulin resistance

Diet does the heavy lifting, but these simple habits help your body respond to insulin better.

🌾 Build on millets

Build meals on millets (foxtail, little, ragi), moong/lentil dosa & idli, or cauliflower rice — they digest slowly and keep blood sugar steady. Pair with dal, veg and curd.

🥗 Half the plate veggies

Thoran, avial, sambar and salads add fiber that slows sugar absorption. Load up on beans, cabbage, carrots, spinach and gourds.

🚶 Walk after meals

A 10–15 minute walk after eating helps muscles pull sugar out of your blood. Easy and surprisingly effective.

🥥 Coconut & protein in moderation

Coconut, fish, chicken, paneer/tofu and dal keep you full and steady. Use coconut oil for flavor, but keep the quantity modest.

💧 Drink water first

Thirst can feel like hunger. Have water or unsweetened sambaram before snacking, and keep water with you all day.

⏱️ Keep meal times steady

Eating at the same four times daily steadies blood sugar and curbs grazing. Try to finish dinner a few hours before bed.

One more time: This plan is educational only and not a substitute for care from your doctor or a registered dietitian. If you feel shaky, dizzy, or unwell after changing how you eat, check in with a medical professional.
